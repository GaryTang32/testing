Technical brief · November 2026

China RSI Adoption

How two Chinese teams put recursive self-improvement (RSI) to work, how the methods operate step by step, and how well they work.

Kimi K3 · Moonshot AIAREX · BAAIEvidence to 25 Oct 2026

Key points
  • Two adoption modes. Moonshot AI builds self-improvement into training (Kimi K3); BAAI applies it at run time (AREX).
  • Effectiveness differs in kind. AREX has component-level proof: its self-audit adds 10.2 points on BrowseComp. K3 has strong outcome results but no published proof that its loops compound.
  • The wider evidence is consistent. Across Chinese research, loops closed by external checks hold their gains; loops where the model only judges itself fade within two to three rounds.

Recommendation: pilot an AREX-style field audit in the extraction agent, keep it only if a with-and-without test shows a gain, and send any use of Chinese models through compliance review (Part 4).

Key terms
Recursive self-improvement (RSI)
An AI system improving the process that improves it, so gains carry forward.
Reinforcement learning (RL)
Training by trial and error, with a reward score as feedback.
Verifier
A check that scores an output, ideally one the model cannot game.
Distillation
Training one model to imitate one or more teacher models.
Mixture of experts (MoE)
A model where each token uses only a few of many expert sub-networks.
Ablation
Running a system with and without one component to measure that component's effect.
Held-out set
Data kept aside to test changes and never used to tune them.
Regression gate
A test suite a change must pass before it is kept.
BrowseComp
A benchmark of hard web-research questions, each stacking several conditions.

What
Open-weights frontier model: 2.8T parameters, 104B active per token, 1M-token context.
RSI use

1.3Methodology, step by step

Use Next and Previous, or jump to a step. Each step shows what happens, the mechanism, the numbers, the control that keeps it honest and how strong the evidence is.

←/→ step · Space play/pause · R replay Step 6 of 6

Overview

Six stages, one loop

K3's training is a pipeline in which the model, or its predecessors, does the work at every stage and a check designed by people closes each one. The last stage feeds back into the first: the model improves the infrastructure that trains the next round.

  1. Seed data: the previous generation teaches the next.
  2. Task synthesis: tasks are generated, not hand-written.
  3. Grading: three kinds of check turn outcomes into reward.
  4. Distillation: nine specialists merge into one model.
  5. Kimi K3: the merged, sparse model.
  6. Recursive link: the model tunes its own kernels.

Step 1 of 6 · Seed data

The previous generation teaches the next

Domain-specialised models from the earlier Kimi series write the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data. People review it before use. The nine RL experts start from this foundation instead of from hand-written examples.

Mechanism
Earlier Kimi models synthesise SFT examples per domain.
Key numbers
Volume not disclosed.
Control
Human-in-the-loop verification of every batch used.
Evidence
DescribedMethod stated in the report; no ablation in our notes.

Step 2 of 6 · Task synthesis

Tasks are generated, not hand-written

Explorer agents browse the web and file what they find into a hierarchical knowledge graph that keeps growing. A synthesis agent retrieves real-world material from the graph and turns it into tasks with checkable outcomes. The experts act on those tasks inside isolated sandboxes.

Mechanism
Explorer agents → self-evolving knowledge graph → synthesis agent → sandboxed task.
Key numbers
51,219,741 sandboxes from 1,505,678 images; checkpoint 133 ms, resume 49 ms.
Control
Each task runs under five agent harnesses (Kimi Code, Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, Hermes), so the model learns the task rather than one tool setup.
Evidence
Scale shownThe effect of task source on scores is not isolated.

Step 3 of 6 · Grading

Three kinds of check turn outcomes into reward

Checkable outcomes go to verifiers, such as tests and the kernel reward ladder. Open-ended work goes to a reward model that writes its own rubric and compares answers in pairs. A hack detector screens every submission, and the verifier stays hidden from the agent.

Mechanism
Verifiers, a rubric-writing judge and a hack detector; agents get a capped number of submissions.
Key numbers
Not disclosed.
Control
Over-long answers lose; faked artifacts score zero; the verifier is hidden.
Evidence
QualitativeHacks were observed and penalised (graph replay, input caching, precision cuts, faked web artifacts); detection rates are not published.

Step 4 of 6 · Distillation

Nine specialists, one student

RL trains nine experts: three domains (general, agentic, coding) at three reasoning-effort levels (low, high, max). One student model then learns from all nine by on-policy distillation: it writes its own answers and is rewarded token by token for agreeing with the relevant teacher.

Mechanism
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation.
Key numbers
9 experts → 1 model. Token reward = clip(log πteacher / πstudent, ±Rmax).
Control
Clipping stops any single token from dominating the update.
Evidence
Outcome onlyDeepSeek V4 (10+ specialists) and GLM-5 use the same pattern; no K3 ablation in our notes.

Step 5 of 6 · Kimi K3

The merged, sparse model

The result routes each token through 16 of 896 experts, which keeps generation cheap enough to run reinforcement learning at this scale.

Mechanism
Sparse mixture of experts; 1M-token context.
Key numbers
2.8T parameters, 104B active. Terminal-Bench 2.1 88.3; BrowseComp 91.2; Artificial Analysis index 57.
Control
Benchmarks are Moonshot-reported; settings vary by benchmark.
Evidence
Strong outcomeTop open-weights model, 4th of 189 overall; about 4–5 months behind the US frontier by an Epoch-based estimate.

Step 6 of 6 · Recursive link

The model improves its own stack

Late in development, an early K3 checkpoint did most of the team's kernel optimisation. Faster kernels make the next round of training and serving cheaper. K3 also wrote a Triton-like compiler and designed a chip in simulation, but neither fed back into its own development.

Mechanism
Kernel optimisation trained as an RL task, then used as real team work.
Key numbers
"The majority" of kernel work; chip of 4 mm² at 100 MHz, designed in one 48-hour run (simulation).
Control
Reward ladder: wrong output = 0, matches the expert kernel = 0.5, near the hardware roofline = up to 1.
Evidence
Not measuredNo published figure for how much these kernels sped up K3's own training, so compounding is unproven.

1.6Core message

Frontier self-improvement is verification engineering.

Contributor, not autonomous

The model does the work in every loop. People design every loop and every check.

Verification is the moat

Gains appear where checks are hard and hidden. Where checks are soft, the model hacks the reward.

Compounding unproven

The outcome is strong, but no published figure shows K3's kernels speeding up its own training.

Takeaway: build the verifier first, hide it from the agent, and anchor scores to a reference result.

AREX

Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI)23 July 2026arXiv 2607.21461Open models

2.1Executive summary

What
Deep-research agent in two sizes: 122B (10B active) and 4B.
RSI use
An outer loop audits each answer by condition, then accepts, refines or restarts, up to five times.
Effectiveness

2.3Methodology, step by step

A standard research loop inside; an audit loop outside with three exits. Step through it.

←/→ step · Space play/pause · R replay Step 6 of 6

Overview

Two loops, three exits

AREX wraps a standard research loop inside a self-audit loop. The inner loop finds a candidate; the outer loop checks it condition by condition and decides whether to accept it, repair it or start again.

  1. Question: every condition must hold.
  2. Research: the inner loop finds a candidate.
  3. Audit: one narrow check per condition.
  4. Decide: accept, refine or restart.
  5. Refine or restart: repair, or start over.
  6. Accept: return the answer, then reset.

Step 1 of 6 · Question

Every condition must hold

BrowseComp questions stack several conditions that together identify one hard-to-find fact. A candidate that meets three of four conditions is still wrong, which is why one research pass is rarely enough.

Mechanism
A multi-condition question as input.
Key numbers
1,266 questions in BrowseComp.
Why it matters
Partial answers score zero, so verifying every condition pays off.

Step 2 of 6 · Research

The inner loop finds a candidate

The agent searches, reads and reasons over many tool calls until it has a candidate answer. On its own, this is a standard deep-research agent.

Mechanism
search → read → reason, repeated.
Key numbers
Inner loop alone: 59.6 on BrowseComp (AREX-Base, 122B total, 10B active).
Evidence
MeasuredBaseline from BAAI's ablation.

Step 3 of 6 · Audit

One narrow check per condition

The outer loop splits the question into its conditions and checks the candidate against each one separately. A narrow question such as "were there exactly three authors?" can be settled with evidence; "is this answer right?" cannot.

Mechanism
Condition-by-condition audit of the candidate.
Control
Decomposition turns one vague judgment into several checkable ones.
Evidence
Partly recordedWhether the audit uses the same model or a separate judge is not in our notes.

Step 4 of 6 · Decide

Three exits

The audit result selects one of three exits: accept the answer, refine the candidate, or restart the research.

Mechanism
Accept, refine or restart.
Key numbers
At most five outer operations per question.
Control
The hard cap bounds cost and latency.
Evidence
Partly recordedThe exact rule for choosing refine over restart is not in our notes.

Step 5 of 6 · Refine or restart

Repair, or start over

Refine keeps the candidate and works on the failing conditions. Restart abandons the line of research when the candidate is far off. Each pass back through the inner loop counts as one outer operation.

Mechanism
Back into the inner loop, with or without the current candidate.
Key numbers
Outer loop: 59.6 → 69.8 (+10.2). Adding context updating: 82.5 (+12.7).
Control
Five-operation cap.
Evidence
MeasuredAblation on BrowseComp only; no per-round curve; what context updating does is not recorded.

Step 6 of 6 · Accept

Return the answer, then reset

When every condition holds, the agent returns the answer. The next question starts from the same agent: on the recorded evidence, nothing learned here carries over.

Mechanism
All conditions pass → answer returned.
Classification
Within-task correction, the roadmap paper's baseline level, not persistent RSI.
What we add
The persistence step: logging failures and feeding them to a gated outer loop (see 2.5).

2.4Effectiveness

BAAI isolated the outer loop's contribution with an ablation. Hover or focus a bar for its value.

What the evidence shows
  • The audit loop alone adds 10.2 points with the model held fixed.
  • A 10B-active agent lands within one point of DeepSeek V4-Pro-Max on BrowseComp.
  • A 4B version reaches 70.7, so the design transfers to small models.
What it does not show
  • Whether the gain holds beyond BrowseComp, which splits unusually cleanly into conditions.
  • How much each extra round adds, or what it costs.
  • Whether gains carry over between questions: they do not, as recorded.

Is it RSI?

Partly. Genuine RSI requires improvements to persist; AREX resets between questions.

Illustrative policy: 4 of 4 pass → accept; 3 of 4 → refine; 2 or fewer → restart. AREX's exact rule is not in our notes. The five-operation cap is from the paper.

Where it sits in our extraction agent

Adoption checklist

1 · Codify conditions

Turn the output schema into explicit per-field checks.

2 · Ask narrow questions

One field at a time, with evidence from the source.

3 · Code first

Deterministic validators where possible; an LLM audit for the rest.

4 · Three exits, hard cap

Accept, re-extract the field, or re-run the document. AREX caps at five.

5 · Ablate the loop

Run with and without the audit on the same held-out set.

6 · Feed errors forward

Field-level failures drive the outer loop's edits.

2.6The message to us

Check one narrow thing at a time, and allow a restart.

Decompose the check

Condition-level questions can be settled with evidence. "Is this right?" cannot.

Restart is a first-class exit

Abandoning a weak line of work beats patching it.

Pair with persistence

Per-task correction does not compound. Feed logged failures to a gated outer loop.

Takeaway: add a field-level audit to the extraction agent, ablate it, then route its failures into the outer loop.

Part 3 · Evidence

Does self-improvement work?

What Chinese research shows across many self-improvement loops, beyond K3 and AREX.

3.1The verification ladder

A 2026 survey of 1,250 self-improvement papers found that gains track how strong the check is. Select a level to see where K3, AREX and other Chinese work sit.

Level 1 · Formal proof

A checker proves the output correct

Definition
A proof assistant or type checker accepts or rejects the result; there is no judgment involved.
Examples here
None of the Chinese systems in this brief use formal checks.
Evidence
Top of the survey's hierarchy: the most durable gains, but only where problems can be formalised.

Level 2 · Execution and external tests

Run it and measure

Definition
Tests, sandboxes, real training runs and benchmark harnesses decide the score.
Examples here
K3's kernel ladder and sandbox outcomes. Self-Harness (Shanghai AI Lab): +14 to +21 points on held-out Terminal-Bench tasks. ASI-Arch (SJTU): architecture discoveries grow roughly linearly with compute.
Evidence
Gains holdSustained, but roughly in proportion to compute rather than accelerating.

Level 3 · Learned judge

A trained model scores the output

Definition
A reward or verifier model, rather than a test, decides the score.
Examples here
K3's rubric-writing judge, which is unaudited. DeepSeekMath-V2's verifier, audited by a meta-verifier: analysis quality 0.85 → 0.96, and human labels dropped in the last two training rounds.
Evidence
ConditionalReliable when the judge itself is checked; otherwise exposed to reward hacking.

Level 4 · Self-assessment

The model judges its own output

Definition
No outside signal; the system decides whether it did well.
Examples here
AREX's condition-level audit (+10.2 on BrowseComp). Continual experience internalization: 23.2% → 8.5% by iteration 3. PKU's ContinualSkillBench: self-built skill libraries scored 0.602 against 0.605 for plain in-context learning.
Evidence
FadesUngrounded self-critique loses about 55% of its informational change over iterations. Splitting the check into narrow questions, as AREX does, helps.

Bar lengths show the survey's ordering, not measured values.

3.3What works and what fails

Works
  • External, hidden checksK3 kernel ladder; Self-Harness tests
  • A regression gate on every self-editSelf-Harness: +14 to +21 points held
  • Testing on held-out dataSelf-Harness gains measured on unseen tasks
  • Splitting a check into narrow questionsAREX: +10.2 BrowseComp
  • Storing lessons in external memoryML-Master 2.0: 72.7% → 54.5% without cross-task memory; ASI-Evolve: 78% of strong designs reused earlier ones
  • Auditing the judgeDeepSeekMath-V2: analysis quality 0.85 → 0.96
Fails
  • The model judging itself over many rounds23.2% → 8.5% by iteration 3
  • Unbounded roundsAgent-World: +6.8 then +1.8
  • No safety test in the loopMisevolution: refusals 99.4% → 54.4%
  • Soft or visible checksK3: faked artifacts, graph replay, input caching
  • Ungated self-rewritingGödel Agent: 14% of runs ended below start

Pattern: self-improvement works where an external, hard-to-game check closes the loop, and fades where the model only judges itself. The roadmap paper "The Last AI Built by Humans" concludes that no system has yet reached genuine RSI.

Sanctions exposure

Recommendation

Use the patterns now. Route any model use through legal and compliance review first.

Conclusion

Chinese labs use self-improvement in two distinct ways. Moonshot builds it into training: K3 helped generate its tasks, grade its answers and tune its kernels, and it is the strongest open-weights model, but whether those loops compound across generations is not measured. BAAI uses it at run time: AREX's self-audit adds a measured 10.2 points on BrowseComp, but its fixes do not carry over between questions.

Across Chinese research the pattern is consistent. Self-improvement works where an external, hard-to-game check closes the loop, and fades where the model only judges itself. The practical step is an AREX-style field audit inside the extraction agent, proven by ablation and connected to a gated outer loop.

Based on research notes dated 25 October 2026. Company figures are self-reported; cross-lab benchmarks use different settings; evidence ratings are our assessment.
